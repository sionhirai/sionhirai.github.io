Valdris: defense procurement intelligence

Every DoD contract action is public, but turning that data into an answer like "who is winning this kind of work, and is it growing?" takes hours of filtering. I built Valdris to answer those questions in seconds.

02 What I built

  • Overview: where the money goes this fiscal year, with a globe that draws arcs from the Pentagon to the states receiving the largest awards.
  • Contract flow and spending trends: which agencies and capability areas are growing, month over month.
  • Competitor intel: who is winning, in which areas, and how their share is moving.
  • Signal feed: open SAM.gov solicitations, with a watchlist saved in your browser.
  • Claude integration: turns recent award activity into a short plain-English summary for readers who don't live in spreadsheets. It's built and wired in, and switched off in production for now.

04 Decisions that mattered

Live data only. Early builds leaned on sample numbers while I worked on the interface. Before launch I audited every figure on the site and removed all of them, so each number now traces back to a public source. For a tool whose audience checks numbers for a living, that was the only defensible choice.

Label what the AI writes. AI summaries are useful but can be wrong, so the panel carries a visible "AI-generated" badge and tells readers to verify figures against the source records. Illustrative views are labeled the same way.

Built for the phone. Most people first see Valdris from a link on a phone, so the redesign added a bottom tab bar, URL routing, and tables that stay readable on a small screen.
